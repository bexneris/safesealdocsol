FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Everything you need to know before your appointment. Don't see your question? Just reach out.

What is a mobile notary?

A mobile notary travels to your location — home, office, hospital, or another agreed meeting place — to witness signatures and notarize documents, instead of you traveling to them.

What is remote online notarization (RON)?

RON lets you notarize a document over a live video call instead of meeting in person. It uses identity verification and a recorded, tamper-evident session, and is only available where state law permits it and once we're connected to a licensed RON platform.

What do I need to bring to my appointment?

A valid, unexpired government-issued photo ID (driver's license, passport, or state ID), and the unsigned document(s). Do not sign in advance — you must sign in front of the notary.

Do you offer same-day or after-hours appointments?

Yes, subject to availability. Same-day, evening, and weekend appointments may include an additional fee — see our pricing page for details.

What areas do you serve?

We travel throughout the Salt Lake City area and surrounding counties within roughly 100 miles. Outside that range, we're often still able to help for an additional travel fee — just ask. Remote online notarization is available anywhere within Utah, subject to state RON rules.

What forms of payment do you accept?

Card payment online through our booking page, or cash, card, or check at the appointment. Loan signings may require a deposit to confirm the booking.

Can a notary give me legal advice?

No. Notaries are not attorneys and cannot give legal advice, help choose which document to use, or explain its legal effect. Please consult an attorney for legal questions about your document.

What if a signer doesn't speak English or needs an interpreter?

Notary rules on interpreters vary by state. Let us know ahead of time so we can confirm what's allowed and arrange accordingly.
